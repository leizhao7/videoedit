We turn movies and their trailers into video-editing tasks, then train multimodal agents to inspect footage, reason about editing decisions, and use editing tools. On held-out films, we study when finer-grained feedback helps—and when it hurts task completion. The project brings together our scalable task-generation pipeline, RL training code, and outcome graders.

02
Task generation

Turn any movie–trailer pair into RL tasks: A scalable data-generation pipeline

The pipeline matches trailer shots to the source movie, annotates the footage, and builds tasks with editing briefs, media inputs, and rubric-based reward functions. Applied to 49 real films, it generates 2,352 tasks; filtering retains 797 (716 training / 81 validation), with no films shared between splits.

The four independent task types are Content Selection (choose shots), Narrative Sequencing (order shots), Rough Cut (set clip boundaries), and Fine Cut (edit dialogue, music, and transitions over fixed picture timing).

Credit assignment
TRAJECTORY-LEVEL

Outcome-only GRPO

A single final task reward supplies the learning signal for all actions in the trajectory.

TURN-LEVEL + TRAJECTORY-LEVEL

GRPO + PRM

An LLM judge scores intermediate actions at each turn, alongside the final task reward. Process-reward weight can decay during training.

TOKEN-LEVEL

On-policy self-distillation (OPSD)

Each generated token receives a distillation signal from a frozen teacher conditioned on reference answers or turn suggestions.

Main experiments: Qwen3.5-9B. Additional GRPO/PRM experiments: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B.

04
Findings

When does finer feedback help—and hurt?

Three findings from our Qwen3.5-9B experiments.

01 / Task-dependent gains

Fine-grained feedback helps more complex tasks.

Decayed process rewards bring the largest benefit on Fine Cut. On the other three task types, outcome-only GRPO remains competitive, with additional gains of at most 10.4%.

+45.4%relative score gain on Fine Cut
02 / Reward hacking

Process rewards can lead to reward hacking.

With a fixed weight, process reward keeps rising while outcome reward declines late in training. Decaying the weight to zero over updates 20–30 mitigates this pattern and improves completion.

44.1%→6.2%validation truncation · fixed → decayed weight
03 / Agent behavior

Finer-grained feedback encourages desirable behaviors.

Decayed process rewards encourage more visual inspection, longer reasoning, and more self-correction markers. These changes accompany the best overall score in the main comparison: 0.447, versus 0.413 for outcome-only GRPO.

1.5–1.9×more inspection, reasoning, and self-correction markers

Scope: main Qwen3.5-9B experiments; held-out scores use avg@4. Task structure, scoring criteria, and horizon vary together. Reward-hacking findings concern our LLM-judged GRPO+PRM setup. Experimental details ↗
